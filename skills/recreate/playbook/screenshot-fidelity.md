## Reading the competitor screenshots
(from taste-skill image-to-code and screenshot-to-code)

Make the section look like the screenshot in structure and rhythm. Study it before writing markup:

- Typography: size relationships between heading, lead and body; weight contrast; how many lines the headline takes; whether headings are calm or loud. Reproduce the hierarchy with h1/h2/h3/.adr-lead, not a flattened generic scale.
- Spacing: headline to lead, text to buttons, gaps between cards, section top and bottom space, side gutters, card padding, image-to-text distance. Keep generous layouts generous and tight layouts tight.
- Components: button shape and fill vs outline, primary vs secondary hierarchy, card structure, dividers, borders, badges, how icons sit in cards, input styling.
- Composition: column count and ratio, where media sits, alignment (left vs centred), what spans full width, where the eye lands first.
- Media: keep consistent aspect ratios for similar modules; images sit in clear frames (.adr-media), not random sizes.

Do not drift into a template while coding. Do not replace a distinctive section with a generic row, merge two different sections into one pattern, or compress generous spacing. When a detail is unclear, keep the visible design language, then the spacing logic, then the component family, and only then choose the simplest faithful version.

Never copy from the screenshot: text, colours, logos, photos, people, or brand marks. Those come from the client.
